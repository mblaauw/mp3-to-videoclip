# Craft

The truth rules still win. These make the true film a good one.

**A motif that travels.** Choose one image the song can carry. Plant it in the first
section, transform it in the middle, pay it off in the last chorus. If it never
changes, it is decoration.

**Section to structure.** A verse states. A chorus returns larger, and the last
chorus holds the largest image in the film. A bridge turns the meaning. An outro
leaves one thing behind.

**A colour script.** One palette and one light per section, written down before any
shot. A change of colour is an event, so spend it where the song changes.

**Lyrics.** Never draw the noun in the line. Draw what the line does to the person.

**Performance in stills.** Hold a performer between two actions: the breath before,
the hand leaving the strings. Never mid-note, never an open mouth at full stretch.

**Refuse these.** Rain on a window. A lone figure on a rooftop. Neon reflections in
a puddle. A hand on glass. A record spinning. A city at night from above. A road at
dawn. A match struck in the dark. A slow-motion hair flick. A crowd with phones up.
A mirror split. An hourglass. A bird taking off. A candle blown out. A page turned.
Use one only when the supplied material demands it, and say so.

**Portrait.** At 9:16, keep the subject in the centre third, leave headroom for
text, and do not build on a horizontal reveal: it will happen outside the frame.

**The sound-off test.** Watch it silently in your head. If the film only makes sense
because the music is playing, the pictures are not doing their job yet.
