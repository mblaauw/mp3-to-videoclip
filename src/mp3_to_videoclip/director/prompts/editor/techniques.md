# Technique library

| Technique | Use it when | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hard cut on a phrase or section boundary | Default join; a new section is a new idea | Inside one continuous idea that should flow |
| Cut on an accent | A drum, stab or vocal entry with high confidence | Accents are low-confidence, or every bar |
| Rhythmic montage | A build into a drop, chorus or reveal | The images need more reading time |
| Breathing hold | A moment should land after a peak or on a key line | The image is static in a high-energy passage |
| Push-in | Intimacy, tension, approaching a reveal | Three in a row; wide busy frames |
| Pull-back | Release, scale or a twist | An opening that needs immediate clarity |
| Lateral pan | A wide frame with information to discover | Close-ups where the crop runs out |
| Match cut | A motif returns, or before/after rhymes | Shapes do not actually align |
| Smash cut | A hard energy or content contrast | Gentle songs, or as a habit |
| Jump cut | Compressed time on the same subject | Different subjects |
| Cross-cutting | Two lines converging or in tension | Only one line of action exists |
| Dissolve | Time passing, memory, a place change | Fast passages or one continuous action |
| Dip to black or white | A chapter break, breath or end card | Mid-phrase |
| Whip pan, push, slide | Energetic or graphic section changes | Calm passages; narrative films that should not feel like slides |
| Punch-in | A big hit or emphasis | More than about once per section |
| Flash or light leak | A strong accent, drop or montage end | More than three flashes in any second |
| Split screen | Comparison or simultaneity | Detailed images that become too small |
| Kinetic type | A short super or lyric hook | Long sentences |
| End card | Every promo or advert | A film that already ended |

A technique is justified by what the viewer feels, not by its name. If two
techniques would do the same job, choose the quieter one.
