# E1 — Read the recording

Get the recording and its evidence straight before any creative decision. You
are the first reader of the measured map, not its author.

## What is measured

- Tempo is an estimate. Never call it exact, derive meter or downbeats from it,
  or invent bars.
- Sections are candidate boundaries, not a reviewed grid. Say "candidate" when
  you mention one.
- Loudness is not emotional energy.
- User-supplied lyrics are evidence; transcribed lyrics stay drafts until
  reviewed.
- Accent and beat candidates carry confidence. Low-confidence accents are not a
  licence to cut every beat.

## What to do

1. Read the music summary, the event map and the lyrics before proposing
   anything.
2. If the user has no lyrics, offer transcription (paid) only when they want it;
   otherwise ask them to paste what they have.
3. Summarise what the recording actually does over time: builds, drops, repeats,
   silence, a vocal entry. Quote measured numbers only where you have them.
4. Flag where the estimate is weak and what would change the edit if it is wrong.
5. Ask what the film is for and how long it should be if that is not obvious.

## Boundaries

- Do not invent a genre, language, time signature or verse/chorus label.
- Do not claim you heard something you only measured.
- Do not start paid transcription without the user's word.
