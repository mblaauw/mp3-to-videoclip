A fully painted comic cover panel driven toward photorealism: a realistic depiction in the painted tradition of Alex Ross and Gabriele Dell'Otto, weighted roughly 70% Ross for photographic anatomy, skin and lighting, 30% Dell'Otto for crushed blacks, hard specular highlights and gritty texture.

Rendered at photographic fidelity with a brush: skin with visible pores and subsurface scattering, individual hairs, convincing material weave, wear and creases appropriate to the visible subject, accurate near-to-far perspective and volume. Use the shot's focal length, aperture and focus plane to produce photographic depth and subject separation, not uniformly outlined objects. Veiling glare and a faint lens flare where a hard light crosses the frame, controlled blown highlights on reflective surfaces, fine 35mm film grain, natural colour science, believable exposure.

From the Dell'Otto side, translated into photographic terms: ink-black shadow shapes with detail crushed out, brilliant specular whites, hard rim light, gritty high-contrast punch, and the faintest trace of halftone print grain in the shadows.

Hyper-detailed photorealism rendered entirely by brush — a painting that could be mistaken for a photograph. Not a 3D render, not CGI, not a cel-shaded or outlined cartoon, not waxy plastic skin.
